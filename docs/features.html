Feature Engineering

Features were engineered from waveform data to capture pulse timing, shape structure, tail behavior, gradient characteristics, and frequency content.

Overview

Why Feature Engineering?

Transforming raw detector signals into machine-learning features.

Each interaction inside the Majorana germanium detectors produces a raw electrical waveform. While these waveforms contain hundreds of time samples, machine learning models often perform better when the signal is summarized into meaningful numerical features.

We therefore extracted physics-motivated features that describe important characteristics of the waveform, including its rise time, tail behavior, gradient structure, and frequency content. These engineered features allow machine learning models to identify patterns that distinguish single-site signal events from background interactions.

Time Domain

Time-Domain Features

Metrics derived from the temporal structure of the waveform's rising edge and drift.

tp0

Identifies the onset of the rising edge of the waveform.

ED

Measures the number of time samples required for the waveform to accumulate 90% of its total energy.

HWP

Measures the temporal width of the waveform between 25% and 75% of its peak amplitude.

AvsE

Calculates the maximum current amplitude divided by the event energy to normalize the steepness of the rising edge.

tdrift10

Counts the number of samples from the onset time to the point where the waveform reaches 10% of its peak amplitude.

tdrift50

Counts the number of samples from the onset time to the point where the waveform reaches 50% of its peak amplitude.

tdrift99

Counts the number of samples from the onset time to the point where the waveform reaches 99.9% of its peak amplitude.

time_to_peak

Measures the number of samples between the waveform onset time and its maximum amplitude.

time_to_main_peak

Measures how quickly the waveform reaches its dominant peak after the initial onset.

Tail

Tail Features

Characteristics of the waveform's decay region after the main pulse peak.

tail_slope

Measures the linear decay rate of the waveform tail, capturing differences in late-time charge dissipation.

LQ80

Quantifies the amount of delayed charge collected after the waveform reaches 80% of its rise.

ND80

Measures the maximum downward deviation below the 80% rise level to identify small notch-like structures.

tfr

Compares the standard deviation of the waveform tail before and after pole-zero correction to measure tail flattening.

PPR

Calculates the ratio of the late-time plateau average to the peak amplitude to identify incomplete charge collection.

Gradient

Gradient Features

Statistics extracted from the derivative of the waveform, capturing shape complexity.

peak_count

Counts the number of significant local maxima in the gradient waveform to identify multi-site events.

gbn

Measures the root mean square of the gradient waveform in the pre-rise baseline region to quantify derivative-level noise.

current_skewness

Measures the asymmetry of the gradient-transformed waveform between the onset time and the peak.

current_kurtosis

Measures the peakedness of the gradient waveform during the rising edge to identify sharp current spikes.

current_width

Measures the temporal width of the gradient waveform near its maximum to determine if charge collection is concentrated.

GradAreaRatio

Compares the area under the largest gradient peak to the second largest peak.

GradWidthMain

Measures the temporal width of the dominant gradient peak.

Frequency Domain

Frequency-Domain Features

Spectral properties extracted via Fourier analysis of the waveform signal.

SCA

Represents the center of mass of the signal's frequency spectrum, calculated as the amplitude-weighted average of frequency components.

spectral_centroid_power

Summarizes where the majority of frequency energy is concentrated by finding the center of mass of the power spectrum.

total_power

Sums all frequency components in the power spectrum to measure overall waveform variability and high-frequency content.

HFER

Measures the concentration of high-frequency energy in the waveform spectrum after normalization.

bpr

Compares the high-frequency power to low-frequency power to capture complex charge structures.
